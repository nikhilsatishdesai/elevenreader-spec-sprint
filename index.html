1 · The product

ElevenReader reads anything aloud, and people use it in the gaps of their day.

ElevenLabs’ consumer audio app turns PDFs, documents, articles and books into natural speech, and includes a library of audiobooks. Creative is one of its biggest growth levers, so the brief I set myself was simple: concepts that earn the install, then the trial.

How it makes money

Free

10 hours of text-to-audio a month (about a 400-page book), 1,000+ voices, sleep timer, sync between web and mobile.

Ultra · $11 a month, 3-day trial

Unlimited imports, 200k+ premium audiobooks, offline downloads and custom voices. So an ad should sell a listening habit that outgrows 10 hours.

2 · Today’s ads

One proven formula, a few things left unsaid.

From ElevenReader’s active ads in Meta’s Ad Library, worldwide, compared with Speechify, Audible and big tech’s notes-to-audio features.

What I looked atWhat ElevenReader doesWhat that leaves open
Where it spendsAbout 1,400 active ads worldwide, around 1,100 of them in the US. Brazil is the one localised market. The UK, Germany and India show almost nothing.Markets beyond the US and Brazil look under-tested.
Format9:16 creator videos: a person holding a phone, talking to camera. Copy is always hook → ✅ checklist → “Download for free”, with a “Listen Now” button.The template wins, so keep it and test the hook. But nobody ever sees where the phone goes.
Hooks“Now, every book comes with a play button.” “Reading feels impossible? Just press play.” Plus social proof and Sir Michael Caine reading The Odyssey.Almost every hook is about books. Your own PDFs and documents, the thing reviewers rave about, sit in the checklist.
Voices“1,000+ voices” as a checklist line, and celebrity narrators.The voice quality is told, not heard. Few ads let you hear one line change character.
CompetitorsSpeechify: celebrity voices, photo scanning, heavy localisation, founder-led. Audible: trial-led. Google and Samsung: “turn your notes into a podcast”, sold as a phone feature.Big tech is already teaching people that documents can become audio. ElevenReader can win on voice quality.

Limits: Meta only. Outside the EU the Ad Library shows no spend or results, so long-running ads with many versions stand in for winners.

3 · Three angles

Each gap becomes an angle, and each angle gets its own concepts.

All three run on broad targeting. Meta’s delivery finds the audience, so the creative angle does the targeting: an ad about documents finds people with documents to get through.

Angle · Your phone, but a book

4 · Video concept A · 9:16 · ~22s

Your phone, but a book.

She never leaves her world. The app appears as translucent panels while the scene softens behind them: offline on the train, CarPlay in the car, a sleep timer in bed. It ends on ElevenReader’s own line: “Listen to anything in voices you’ll love.”

  • Only the first three seconds change between the three cuts, so any difference in hook rate comes from the hook.
  • A warm, unhurried narrator instead of an ad read, mixed so every line sits at the same loudness.
Angle · One sentence, four voices

Video concept B · 9:16 · 27s

One sentence, four voices.

“Someone knocked four times. Nobody was there.” Read as noir, bedtime, horror and play-by-play. The story stays the same; only how it feels changes, which is the whole product in 27 seconds.

  • ElevenReader’s own visual language: the gradient voice orb, the frosted voice card and the word-by-word highlight.
  • Ends on “Same story. Pick how it feels.”

Static concepts · 1080 × 1350

Six statics, two per angle.

Built on ElevenLabs’ own ad system: calm grounds, two-tone type and one product moment each. They run in Feed as single images and as a three-card carousel, one card per angle.

5 · Launch and test plan

Find the hook cheaply, then pay for installs.

Broad targeting, one variable per ad set, equal budgets per cell, and every decision made against a rule written down before launch. Nothing below has been run: it is the plan I would bring to the performance team.

The campaigns

PhasePlatform and placementObjectiveCreativeWhat it answers
Weeks 1–2Hook testMeta: Reels and StoriesAwareness (ThruPlay)Video A hooks A, B and C · Video BWhich opening stops the scroll, and which story holds attention to the end.
Weeks 1–2Angle testMeta: Feed, single image and a three-card carouselTraffic (outbound clicks to the store)The six statics · the carouselWhich angle earns the click: phone moments, voices, or your own reading.
Weeks 3–4Install testMeta: Advantage+ app campaign, all placementsApp promotion (installs, optimising for trial starts)The best two videos and best three statics from phase 1Which concept brings installs that start a trial, not just installs.
Weeks 5–6ExpandGoogle App campaigns (YouTube Shorts and in-stream) · Reddit (r/audiobooks, r/books, productivity communities)App installs on Google · Traffic on RedditPhase 3 winners, resized · voice and reading statics for RedditWhether the winners carry to other channels and their audiences.
After week 6Scale and iterateWinning channels, plus the UK and GermanyInstallsNew hooks on the winning angle · localised versionsHow far the winner scales, and whether under-tested markets respond.

The metrics to watch

MetricHow it’s measuredWhat it tells usStarting rule
Hook rate3-second video views ÷ impressionsWhether the first three seconds stop the scrollAfter 5,000 impressions, replace hooks under about 25%
Hold rateThruPlays ÷ 3-second viewsWhether the story keeps people watchingCompare within the same video; fix the weakest scene first
Outbound CTROutbound clicks ÷ impressionsWhether the angle creates real intentRank angles after at least 50 clicks per cell
Cost per outbound clickSpend ÷ outbound clicksWhat that intent costsUse with CTR to pick what moves to phase 3
CPISpend ÷ installsHow efficiently a concept installsScale cells that beat the account’s baseline CPI; cut the bottom third weekly
Trial start rateUltra trial starts ÷ installsWhether the ad’s promise matches the productA cheap install with no trials is a losing concept
D1 / D7 retention · trial to paidReturning users · paying users ÷ trialsWhether the concept brought the right peopleThe final judge of a winning angle
Frequency and fatigueAverage frequency · CTR versus its peakWhen a winner needs a refreshRefresh when frequency passes 3 or CTR falls 20% from its peak

The thresholds are starting points, to be recalibrated against ElevenReader’s own baselines in the first week. Creative analytics (for example in Motion) would tag each ad by angle, hook and format so learnings roll up across tests, not just within one.

6 · Social

Two accounts, what’s working and what isn’t.

The application asks for this, so here is the short version. I looked at the last month of ElevenLabs on X and YouTube.

X · @ElevenLabs · 206k followers

Working

  • Model launches travel: Eleven v4 reached about 5M views and 19k likes.
  • Human stories land: Baroness Campbell speaking in the House of Lords with an AI voice drew 186k views and 1.4k likes, the best like rate of the month.

Not working

  • Corporate news (hiring, summit attendance, certifications) stalls at 5–25k views.
  • The consumer app barely appears: ElevenReader’s v4 post reached 27k.
YouTube · @elevenlabs · 230k subscribers

Working

  • Flagship launches and events: Eleven v4 (250k views), the $100k “Search” competition (101k), Recast (72k).
  • Full-workflow tutorials keep a steady audience.

Not working

  • Shorts sit at 1–10k views, and most announce other companies’ models (“Seedance 2.5 landed in ElevenCreative”), not what only ElevenLabs can do.

What I’d do more of

Show the voice, don’t announce the feature.

A recurring Shorts and Reels series built on what only ElevenLabs does: one line, performed four ways in Eleven v4, 15 seconds each, ending with how to make it yourself. Alongside it, 30-second creator stories from real ElevenReader users (the dad who listens while doing chores, the student with ADHD) for the consumer side the feeds rarely show.

TikTok isn’t available in India, where I’m based, so I picked the two platforms I could study properly.

7 · How it was made

One Flow, reusable as a template.

Generate

ElevenCreative Flow

Voices, music, sound effects, images and Veo video shots in a single Flow. Swap the script or the character and re-run to get a new cell.

Design

Figma

Video B’s motion design (the voice orb, the frosted voice cards and the reader screen) and the end card.

Edit

DaVinci Resolve

One timeline per cell: cuts, translucent panels, captions, and a mix with every line at the same loudness.

About 20,400 ElevenLabs credits end to end (about ₹360), with every generation price-checked before it ran.
