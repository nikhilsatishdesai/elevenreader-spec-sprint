Research

What ElevenReader’s ads don’t say yet.

From ElevenReader’s live ads in Meta’s Ad Library, worldwide, compared with Speechify. Every ad runs the same formula: a hook, a ✓ checklist, “Download for free”.

01 · Audience

Nobody speaks to students.

Not one student or exam ad from either app, yet students arrive every exam season with reading they already own.

02 · Product

The phone is never shown.

Offline on the train, CarPlay in the car, a sleep timer in bed: real features that never appear in the ads.

03 · Craft

Voices are told, not heard.

“1,000+ voices” is a checklist line. The fastest proof is hearing one sentence change character.

Test plan

Three angles, one variable per cell.

Your notes, in your earsStudents and exam-takers, worldwide · 2 staticsHypothesis: the urgency headline (“Exam in 14 days.”) beats the reframe on CTR in exam season.
Your phone, but a bookPeople who scroll more than they read · Video A + 2 staticsHypothesis: the “no signal” hook wins hook rate with commuters; “screen time” wins CTR.
One sentence, four voicesFiction and audiobook listeners · Video B + 2 staticsHypothesis: hearing the voice change beats being told about it, so Video B wins hold rate and CTR.

Week 1 tests angles. Week 2 tests hooks on the winning angle. Week 3 tests formats.

Video concept A · 9:16 · ~22s

Your phone, but a book.

She never leaves her world. The app appears as translucent panels while the scene softens behind them: offline on the train, CarPlay in the car, a sleep timer in bed.

  • Only the hook changes between the three cuts, so any difference in hook rate comes from the hook.
  • One voice, one take, mixed in DaVinci Resolve to −14 LUFS with every line within about 1 dB.

Video concept B · 9:16 · 27s

One sentence, four voices.

“Someone knocked four times. Nobody was there.” Read as noir, bedtime, horror and play-by-play. The story stays the same; only how it feels changes.

  • ElevenReader’s own language: the gradient voice orb, the frosted voice card and the word-by-word highlight.
  • Ends on “Same story. Pick how it feels.”
